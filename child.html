Child mental illness & neurodevelopmental disorders

The curriculum's fifth item ("Be familiar with child's mental illness"). Separation anxiety is covered on the Anxiety disorders page; here are the four the CTEVT curriculum and the Mental Health-3 class notes name: intellectual disability, ADHD, autism, and the behaviour disorders.

Intellectual Disability (Intellectual Developmental Disorder)

CTEVT PSC curriculum319 · F70–F79

CTEVT PSC curriculum: on the official Psychosocial Counselor syllabus, can appear in the exam. Beyond curriculum: added for completeness, not examinable. A chip like 296.2x · F32 is the diagnosis code: the DSM-5's older ICD-9-CM number, then the ICD-10 "F-code" used in hospital records. Non-students can ignore all of these.
DSM-5 Criteria: all three required
  1. Deficits in intellectual functions: reasoning, problem-solving, planning, abstract thinking, judgment, academic learning, learning from experience: confirmed by clinical assessment and standardised testing (IQ roughly ≤ 70).
  2. Deficits in adaptive functioning across three domains: conceptual (language, literacy, number, memory), social (empathy, social judgment, friendships, gullibility), practical (self-care, money, safety, work, transport).
  3. Onset during the developmental period (childhood or adolescence; the class notes and CTEVT say "before age 18").

Severity, mild / moderate / severe / profound, is graded by adaptive functioning and support needed, not IQ alone. Causes: genetic, prenatal, perinatal, and postnatal.

ID is a permanent developmental state, not an episodic illness. The counselor's work is family psychoeducation, skill-building with caregivers, disability-card and special-education advocacy, and caregiver support.

ADHD · Autism · ODD · Conduct Disorder

CTEVT PSC curriculum

CTEVT PSC curriculum: on the official Psychosocial Counselor syllabus, can appear in the exam. Beyond curriculum: added for completeness, not examinable. A chip like 296.2x · F32 is the diagnosis code: the DSM-5's older ICD-9-CM number, then the ICD-10 "F-code" used in hospital records. Non-students can ignore all of these.
ADHD: 314.0x · F90.x
  1. ≥6 inattention symptoms, and/or ≥6 hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms, for ≥6 months (≥5 from age 17):
    • Inattention: careless mistakes · can't sustain attention · doesn't seem to listen · fails to finish · disorganised · avoids sustained mental effort · loses things · distractible · forgetful
    • Hyperactivity-impulsivity: fidgets · leaves seat · runs/climbs · can't play quietly · "driven by a motor" · talks excessively · blurts answers · can't wait turn · interrupts
  2. Several symptoms present before age 12.
  3. Present in 2 or more settings (home and school).
  4. Clear interference with functioning; and
  5. Not better explained by another disorder.
Autism Spectrum Disorder: 299.00 · F84.0
  1. Persistent deficits in social communication and interaction: all 3: social-emotional reciprocity · nonverbal communication · developing and understanding relationships.
  2. Restricted, repetitive behaviour: ≥2 of 4: stereotyped movements/speech (lining up toys, echolalia) · insistence on sameness and routines · highly restricted, intense interests · sensory hyper- or hypo-reactivity.
  3. Present in the early developmental period.
  4. Clinically significant impairment.
  5. Not better explained by intellectual disability alone.

Oppositional Defiant Disorder (313.81/F91.3): ≥6 months of angry or irritable mood, argumentative-defiant behaviour toward authority, or vindictiveness; ≥4 symptoms, with someone other than a sibling.

Conduct Disorder (312.8x/F91.x): a more severe pattern violating others' basic rights or major norms; ≥3 of 15 behaviours in 12 months across four groups: aggression to people and animals, destruction of property, deceitfulness or theft, and serious rule violations. ODD can precede CD; CD with callous-unemotional traits carries the highest risk.

Nepal

In Nepali classrooms the ADHD child is "badmash", the autistic child "lato", the child with ID "kamjor": moral labels where clinical ones belong; childhood depression hides behind stomachaches and irritability. School screening and teacher psychoeducation are high-leverage counselor tasks. Disability identity cards (four classes: red, blue, yellow, white) unlock allowances and school support: helping a family obtain one is a concrete counseling outcome.

A worked example: A 9-year-old named Kiran fidgets constantly, blurts answers, and cannot finish assignments. His teacher calls him "badmash," but a counselor identifies six inattention and six hyperactivity symptoms present before age 12, in both home and school, confirming the diagnosis. The intervention focuses on task breakdown and positive reinforcement rather than moral correction.

A skill to try · two minutes

Co-regulation: lend your calm. A distressed child borrows an adult's nervous system. Get low to their level, slow your own breathing so they can hear it, and name the feeling: "you are angry, the tower fell". A named feeling shrinks; a lectured feeling grows.

A coping skill, not a treatment: it supports care, it does not replace it.

Key points
  1. Intellectual disability is a lifelong developmental state, graded by the support a person needs, not by IQ alone.
  2. ADHD, autism, ODD and conduct disorder each have a distinct pattern; moral labels ('badmash', 'lato') hide clinical ones.
  3. Children show depression as stomachaches and irritability; school screening and helping families get disability cards are high-impact work.
