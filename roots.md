# Roots: from philosophy to psychology

Psychology is a young science with a very old past. For more than two thousand years before the first laboratory, the questions of this whole site, what is mind? why do people suffer? how do they heal?, belonged to philosophers, physicians, and priests, in Asia as much as in Greece.

## What is psychology?

**Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behaviour**: of what people think, feel, and do, and why. Its four classic goals: *describe* behaviour, *explain* it, *predict* it, and *help change* it. It earns the word "scientific" by testing claims with evidence, observation, surveys, case studies, and experiments, rather than by authority or tradition.

| Don't confuse | Who they are | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psychologist | Studied psychology (MA/M.Phil/PhD) | Assessment, psychotherapy, testing; no medicines |
| Psychiatrist | Medical doctor (MD Psychiatry) | Diagnoses and prescribes medicines; your referral point for severe illness |
| Psychosocial counselor | Trained counselor (e.g., this 780-hour CTEVT course) | Detects, supports, counsels, refers: the community's first line |
| Traditional healer (dhami-jhankri) | Community spiritual healer | First contact for many families; collaborate, don't compete |

| Goal | The question it asks | In counselling practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Describe | What exactly is happening? | Naming the pattern: racing heart, avoidance, sleepless nights, for three weeks |
| Explain | Why is it happening? | Linking the panic to an over-active alarm system and learned avoidance ([Fear: the foundation](https://manoatlas.com/foundation.md)) |
| Predict | What will happen next? | Knowing that exam season, a new stressor, or stopping medicine raises relapse risk |
| Control, or help change | What can we do about it? | Psychoeducation, exposure, activity scheduling, a safety plan |

Main branches you will meet: clinical & counselling (this course's home), developmental ([Human development](https://manoatlas.com/development.md)), social, cognitive, biological/neuro ([Fear: the foundation](https://manoatlas.com/foundation.md)), and community psychology ([Wellbeing & intervention](https://manoatlas.com/wellbeing.md)).

**A worked example:** A 20-year-old student named Maya keeps skipping lectures and says she feels "empty." Using the four goals of psychology, her counselor describes her behaviour (describe), links it to a pattern of avoidance (explain), predicts that isolation will worsen it without intervention (predict), and sets up structured engagement activities (help change).

## The pre-psychology era

| Era & thinkers | The idea | Why it still matters to a counselor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient healing traditions | Distress caused by spirits, gods, karma, or imbalance; treated by ritual, trepanation, exorcism | This worldview is not "the past": it is the first explanation many Nepali clients still receive |
| Hindu & Buddhist psychology (Upanishads from ~800 BCE; Abhidhamma from ~300 BCE) | Systematic maps of mind (man, buddhi, chitta, ahamkara); suffering (dukkha) arises from craving and can be worked with through awareness and discipline | Mindfulness-based therapies descend from this line; the Nepali "man" (heart-mind) you counsel through is part of it |
| Ayurveda & Greek humoral medicine (Hippocrates, ~400 BCE; Charaka, ~100 BCE–200 CE) | Mental illness as bodily imbalance, three dosha (vata, pitta, kapha) or four humours, not divine punishment | The first naturalistic theory of mental illness: disorder as illness, not sin |
| Greek philosophy; Socrates, Plato, Aristotle | Reason examines the self ("know thyself"); Plato: mind separate from body; Aristotle: soul as the form of the body, memory and association | The talking cure begins here: the belief that examining one's own thoughts can change one's life |
| Stoics (Epictetus, ~100 CE) | "People are disturbed not by things, but by the views they take of them" | Quoted almost word-for-word as the founding idea of CBT |
| Descartes (1600s) | Mind-body dualism; the body as machine | Gave us the mind/body split that conversion disorder and psychosomatic medicine have been repairing ever since |
| Empiricists: Locke, Hume (1600s–1700s) | Mind as blank slate (tabula rasa); knowledge from experience and association | The seed of behaviourism and of learning-based therapy: what was learned can be relearned |
| Moral treatment; Pinel, Tuke (1790s) | Unchaining asylum patients; illness treated with dignity and routine | The direct ancestor of psychosocial care, and of the fight against chaining that Nepal still fights |

## Psychology becomes a science: the schools

In **1879 Wilhelm Wundt opened the first psychology laboratory** in Leipzig, Germany: the conventional birthday of scientific psychology. The schools that followed each kept one piece of the truth, and each gave counselling one of its tools:

| School | Founder(s) | Core claim | Gift to counselling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Structuralism | Wundt · Titchener | Break consciousness into elements by introspection | The habit of careful observation |
| Functionalism | William James | Ask what the mind is *for*: adaptation | "What is this symptom doing for the client?" |
| Psychoanalysis | Freud | Unconscious conflict, childhood, defence mechanisms | The psychodynamic approach; the idea that talking heals |
| Behaviourism | Pavlov · Watson · Skinner | Behaviour is learned: conditioning and reinforcement | Exposure therapy; the reinforcement loop of [Fear: the foundation](https://manoatlas.com/foundation.md) |
| Humanistic | Rogers · Maslow | People grow toward their potential given empathy, genuineness, unconditional positive regard | The core counselling attitude itself: Rogers' conditions ARE basic counselling |
| Cognitive | Beck · Ellis (1950s–60s) | Thoughts stand between events and feelings | CBT: the most-used approach in this curriculum |
| Socio-cultural / biopsychosocial | Vygotsky · Engel (1977) | Mind develops in culture; illness is biological + psychological + social at once | The lens this whole atlas uses, and the reason "psychosocial" is in your job title |

**The thread to hold:** every school survives inside your practice. When you normalise a body reaction, you are a biologist; when you trace a thought, a cognitivist; when you set an exposure ladder, a behaviourist; when you listen without judgment, a humanist; when you ask what the family and the village believe, you are doing what no laboratory can, and that is the psychosocial part.

Key points

1. Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behaviour; it began as philosophy and became a science in Wundt's lab in 1879.
2. Asia's traditions (Ayurveda, Upanishads, Buddhist psychology) named the mind and its suffering long before Europe did.
3. Each school (psychoanalysis, behaviourism, humanistic, cognitive) left a tool you still use in counselling today.
