# Crosswalk content review: reviewer-delta

Reviewer: reviewer-delta (cold read, 2026-09-25). Scope: `content/crosswalk.html`, `keypoints/crosswalk.html`, `quizzes/crosswalk.html`, checked against `review/curriculum-page-plan.md` (approved plan v2) and `review/curriculum-coverage.md` (read-only audit). `python3 build.py` run once, clean. No files edited, nothing committed.

**Verdict: AGREE-WITH-CHANGE.** The rebuild does what the plan asked: the map is now a real module-to-page map, every hours and marks figure I could check matches the audit exactly, no invented figures, no coverage percentages, no em-dashes, no links to the handbook or the roadmap, and the three quiz questions are warm with a correct `data-a` on each. ## Findings

1. **must-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:112` and `:113`, `keypoints/crosswalk.html:1`, `quizzes/crosswalk.html` (Q3 feedback).** The page claims total coverage that its own table denies. English: "Everything the PDF examines is findable here, by topic or by search." Key points: "Search or use the map and you will find any topic." Quiz feedback: "search finds any topic either way." The table immediately above marks Facilitation Skill (59 h) "not yet on the site" with no page at all, and the audit records 33 MISSING units out of 159, 27 of them in Module 4 alone. A student who believes this sentence skips the largest hole on the page. Fix: scope the claim to the atlas, not the PDF. English suggestion: "Every topic in the table above is findable here, by topic or by search. The rows marked partly or not yet on the site are the ones to take to your trainer." Nepali line 113 carries the same claim ("पीडीएफले जे परीक्षा लिन्छ, सबै यहाँ विषयले वा खोजले भेटिन्छ।") and needs the same rewrite.

2. **must-fix · `keypoints/crosswalk.html:1` (en) and the matching `<li>` in the same line (ne).** "Every module and sub-module of the PSC course maps to a page here, honestly marked: covered, partly, or not yet on the site." One sub-module maps to no page: Facilitation Skill, whose "Here on the atlas" cell is a lone middot, not a link. Nepali: "हरेक मोड्युल र उप-मोड्युल यहाँको कुनै पृष्ठसँग जोडिएको छ". Fix: "Every module and sub-module of the PSC course appears in the table below, honestly marked: covered, partly, or not yet on the site." Swap "maps to a page" for "appears in the table", and mirror it in Nepali.

3. **must-fix · `content/crosswalk.html`, absent between the expander table and the "Why the atlas order" heading.** The approved plan (item 4) requires "The PDF's two-numbering note is a one-line bilingual footnote beside this table, stating the site follows the course-structure numbering." The audit (section 10, error 7) requires the same thing: "The crosswalk should state that the PDF itself uses two numberings and name which one the site follows." Neither is there, and the omission is now visible to a reader. This page numbers Psychosocial Intervention as Module 1; `content/anxiety.html` still prints "M2 · Sub-module 2 · Task 3" and `content/basics.html` still prints "M2 · Mental Health · Tasks 1–2". A student who follows the Mental Health row to basics meets "M2" against this table's "Module 1" with no explanation. Fix: add the one-line bilingual footnote, for example "The curriculum PDF numbers its modules two ways in different places. This atlas follows the course-structure table, where Psychosocial Intervention is Module 1. A few older pages still show the other numbering." (Nepali: "पाठ्यक्रम पीडीएफले मोड्युलको नम्बर दुई तरिकाले दिएको छ। यो एट्लासले पाठ्य-संरचना तालिकाको नम्बर अपनाएको छ, जसमा मनोसामाजिक हस्तक्षेप मोड्युल १ हो। केही पुराना पृष्ठले अर्को नम्बर देखाउँछन्।") Then fix the two page labels so the note can be dropped later.

4. **must-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:12` (and `:36`).** Module 1, "Wellbeing, Intervention &amp; CBI (20 h)" is marked "covered here", but the audit records 2 of its 5 tasks COVERED and 3 PARTLY (task 2 psychosocial intervention, task 3 basic rights and psychosocial work, task 4 multidisciplinary holistic approach). Three of those PARTLY items are the audit's ranked gap number 8: "Rights and roles (SM1 task 3, task 2): human rights, women's rights, CRC... counsellor vs social worker vs case manager; qualities of a PS worker; Galappatti's types and principles of intervention." The row also applies the status inconsistently: Special Issues (`:16`) has 4 of 8 tasks COVERED and is marked "partly", while this row with 2 of 5 is marked "covered here". Fix: change this status to "partly" and apply a clear majority rule across the table (see finding 5).

5. **should-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:58`.** The rollup rule from task status to sub-module status is never stated, and the reader is told only what one of the three labels means: `"Partly" means the topic is named here, not taught at curriculum depth.` Nothing defines "covered here" or "not yet on the site", so a student cannot tell how much depth "covered here" promises. The statuses as applied also float between thresholds: "covered here" appears at 2 of 5 tasks (SM1 Wellbeing), 5 of 6 (M3 Approaches), 11 of 17 (M3 Communication Skills) and 23 of 24 (M3 Counseling Process), while "partly" appears at 4 of 8 (M1 Special Issues) and 3 of 5. A plain majority rule ("covered here" only when most of the sub-module's tasks are COVERED in the audit) flips exactly one row, the one in finding 4, and leaves the rest alone. Fix: state the rule in the same paragraph, for example: `"Covered here" means the atlas teaches most of that sub-module. "Partly" means the topic is named here, not taught at curriculum depth. "Not yet on the site" means nothing is written yet.` (Nepali mirror needed: the current ne line explains only «आंशिक» too.)

6. **should-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:14` and `:16` (en), `:38` and `:40` (ne).** The map puts `{{page:gbv}}` under Module 1 "(Gender,) Culture &amp; Counselling" and leaves it out of the Special Issues row. The audit places it the other way: "Gender and GBV: sex vs gender, roles and needs, equity/equality, sexuality, types, causes, support (rights-based) | COVERED | gbv" is task 4 of SM5 Special Issues, and SM3's only covered task is "Culture in counselling", which the audit places on nepal. The "(Gender,)" in the sub-module title is the likely cause of the slip. Fix: move gbv from the SM3 row to the Special Issues row in both tables.

7. **should-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:30` and `:54`.** The Facilitation Skill row uses `<span class="pill beyond">`, the grey pill the whole site reserves for "Beyond curriculum". The home page legend defines that tag: "**Beyond curriculum** marks content added for completeness (the rest of the DSM-5, newer research, practical extras): worth knowing, not examinable." So grey means "not examinable" to every reader who has been on the site before, and this page's own explainer at `:58` says the same: "A few pages sit beyond the exam syllabus... and carry the "beyond curriculum" tag". The row it marks is 59 examinable hours. The label text ("not yet on the site" / "अझै साइटमा छैन") is right; the colour says the opposite of the truth. Note that `pill.todo` and `pill.beyond` are also repurposed on this page (teal = "partly", grey = "not yet on the site") where site-wide teal means "CTEVT PSC curriculum". Fix: give the three statuses their own classes or colours so the code does not collide with the syllabus/beyond legend, and add a one-line legend for the three pills under the table.

8. **should-fix · `content/crosswalk.html:35-55` (ne table).** Seven Nepali module and sub-module names appear on this page for the first time and are used nowhere else on the site: "मनोसामाजिक हस्तक्षेप", "परामर्श सीप र प्रक्रिया", "क्षमता विकास", "सहजीकरण-सीप", "व्यावसायिकता विकास", "दस्तावेजीकरण", "विशेष विषयहरू". (By contrast "केस व्यवस्थापन" and "रेफरल" and "संवाद" already exist on the site, and those are reused correctly.) The glossary sets the opposite rule: "The English term is kept because it is what you will hear in hospitals, referral letters and exams; the Nepali column gives the meaning, not a coined translation." The repo notes also say not to coin Sanskritised Nepali for a technical term with no natural equivalent, and to transliterate instead. "सहजीकरण-सीप" and "व्यावसायिकता विकास" are the clearest cases. Whether Nepali PSC colleges already use these renderings is unverified, so this is Pravo's call, not mine. Fix: either keep the English module names in the Nepali table (glossary rule), or add the Nepali terms to the glossary so a reader meets them defined the first time.

14. **nit · `content/crosswalk.html:113`.** "पीडीएफले जे परीक्षा लिन्छ, सबै यहाँ विषयले वा खोजले भेटिन्छ।" has a subject and verb that do not agree ("विषयले ... भेटिन्छ"). That covers Module 4 (59 + 16 = 75 against a printed 73) without inventing a total, which is what the plan asked for.
