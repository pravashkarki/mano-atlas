# Beyond the curriculum: what a counsellor needs that the CTEVT PSC course leaves out

Research pass, 2026-09-11. Read-only on the repo. Input for the start-to-finish table of contents.

**What this is:** the second half of the owner's goal. `review/curriculum-coverage.md` maps the 2010 CTEVT PSC curriculum. This file lists what a psychologist or psychosocial counsellor needs that the curriculum does not contain, benchmarked against published frameworks, with a verified source for each topic.

**How to read the tables**

- **In curriculum?** none, or partly with the curriculum unit (official numbering: M1 Psychosocial Intervention, M2 Case Management, M3 Counseling Skill and Process, M4 Capacity Building; SM = sub-module, T = task).
- **On site now:** what the 33 live chapters already say (checked by grep of `content/`, 2026-09-11).
- **Priority:** CORE (every practitioner), IMPORTANT, OPTIONAL.
- **Sources:** keys like [S12] point to the source register at the end. Every key was checked this session; its verification status is in the register.

Two things the benchmarks agree on that the curriculum has almost none of: **scientific literacy** (APA Goal 2, CACREP research area) and **the brief evidence-based interventions** that non-specialists now deliver worldwide (the WHO series). The curriculum's strength, skills and process, is exactly what ENACT and EQUIP measure, so the site's practice group is a good base to build on.

## 2. Diagnostic-system update the site should know about

The site cites DSM-5 throughout. Not a new chapter, but a site-wide accuracy note for the main session.

| Change | Source | What it touches on the site |
|---|---|---|
| DSM-5-TR (March 2022) adds **prolonged grief disorder** to trauma- and stressor-related disorders: grief causing distress or impairment for more than 12 months after the death. | S16 | trauma, a future grief chapter |
| DSM-5-TR adds ICD-10-CM symptom codes for **suicidal behaviour and nonsuicidal self-injury**. | S16 | suicide |
| DSM-5-TR renames intellectual disability to **intellectual developmental disorder**; reinstates **unspecified mood disorder**; clarifies criteria for more than 70 disorders; revises text for the impact of racism and discrimination. | S16 | child (ID section title), depression, basics |
| ICD-11 adds **complex PTSD**, **prolonged grief disorder** (beyond about 6 months, so shorter than DSM-5-TR's 12) and **gaming disorder**; a WHO diagnostic manual (CDDR) followed in March 2024. | S17 | trauma, substance, a grief chapter |
| ICD-11 moves **gender incongruence** out of the mental disorders chapter into "conditions related to sexual health". | S19 | rest (gender dysphoria row) |
| ICD-11 replaces personality disorder types with **severity (mild, moderate, severe) plus trait domains** (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia) and an optional borderline pattern qualifier. | S20 | rest (personality row) |
| ICD-11 lists **burn-out** as an occupational phenomenon (code QD85, in "factors influencing health status"), explicitly not a medical condition. | S18 | ethics (self-care card) |

Suggested handling: one short "How diagnosis works: DSM-5-TR and ICD-11" page at the head of the disorders group (topic D1 below), and a sweep replacing "DSM-5" with "DSM-5-TR" where criteria are quoted. Criteria text changed for some disorders, so each quoted criterion needs a check against DSM-5-TR before the label changes.

**Fact-check note found in passing.** The suicide page says police data undercounts "because of stigma and the law's history". A 2022 legal analysis found there has never been criminal liability for attempted suicide in Nepal; the belief that there is one is what feeds underreporting [S27]. Abetment of suicide is an offence under section 185 of the National Penal Code 2017 (secondary sources). Worth rewording to "because of stigma and a widespread but mistaken belief that attempting suicide is a crime".

## 4. Rejected, or mention only with a caveat

| Practice or claim | Decision | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-session psychological debriefing / critical incident stress debriefing | REJECT | WHO: "should not be used" after recent trauma (strong recommendation); Lilienfeld lists CISD as probably harmful (raised PTSD risk in RCTs). The site's pfa page should say so if it does not already. | S34, S47 |
| Benzodiazepines for acute traumatic stress or sleep in the first month | Outside counsellor scope; mention as a fact | WHO 2013: should not be offered; may slow recovery and cause dependence. | S34 |
| Grief counselling offered routinely to people with normal grief | CAVEAT | Listed as possibly harmful (increased depressive symptoms in a meta-analysis). Offer support and watch for prolonged grief instead. | S47 |
| Kübler-Ross stages as a sequence the bereaved must pass through | CAVEAT | Useful as history; prescribing stages misguides the bereaved (Stroebe, Schut, Boerner 2017). Teach the dual process model and Worden's tasks. | S37 |
| Emotional Freedom Techniques (tapping) | CAVEAT, do not teach as a technique | Named in the curriculum's DACUM list. Proponent meta-analyses report effects; an independent re-analysis found the claim that acupoint tapping is the active ingredient unsupported. Any benefit is likely the exposure and attention parts. Record the decision in `intake.md`. | S67 |
| Draw-a-person / human figure drawings as a test of personality or abuse | CAVEAT | Drawing is a fine engagement activity; as an assessment its validity evidence is very limited. | S66 |
| Neuro-linguistic programming (NLP) | REJECT | Review of 35 years of research: most studies do not support NLP's tenets. | S68 |
| Learning styles (visual, auditory, kinaesthetic) in training design | REJECT | No evidence that matching teaching to a "style" improves learning. Matters for the M4 facilitation chapters. | S65 |
| Myers-Briggs type as a clinical or selection tool | CAVEAT | Psychometric limits; fine as a conversation starter, not as assessment. | S72 |
| Coercive "attachment therapy", holding therapy, rebirthing | REJECT | Contraindicated: risk of serious harm, no proven benefit (APSAC 2006); deaths reported. | S69, S47 |
| Recovered-memory techniques, repeated suggestive questioning, hypnosis to "find" memories | REJECT | Can produce false memories of trauma (possibly harmful list). | S47 |
| Therapy that encourages "alter" personalities (DID-oriented therapy) | CAVEAT | Listed as possibly harmful; relevant to the dissociative chapter (D8). | S47 |
| Conversion or "reparative" practices for sexual orientation or gender identity | REJECT | UN Independent Expert (2020): severe harm, can amount to torture, calls for a global ban; APA affirmative guidelines (2021). | S70, S43 |
| Facilitated communication / Rapid Prompting Method for autistic or non-speaking people | REJECT | ASHA 2018: discredited; messages come from the facilitator; documented harms including false abuse allegations. | S71, S47 |
| Scared Straight, boot camps, fear-based and information-only drug education (DARE-type) | REJECT for awareness work | Probably harmful: worsened conduct or increased substance use in trials. Directly relevant to M4 awareness programmes. | S47 |
| Peer-group interventions that put conduct-disordered youth together | CAVEAT | Possibly harmful (deviancy training). Mix groups or use parent-focused work. | S47 |
| The "critical positivity ratio" and other pop positive-psychology formulas | REJECT the formula; CAVEAT the field | The 2.9013 ratio was shown to be mathematically unfounded; many social-psychology findings replicate weakly. | S63, S64 |
| "Depression is a serotonin deficiency" as a simple cause | CAVEAT | Umbrella review found no consistent support for low serotonin as the cause (contested methods); separately, antidepressants outperform placebo on average. Do not use either claim to argue a client on or off medicine. | S76, S75 |
| EMDR, TF-CBT delivered by a PSC | Outside scope; referral only | WHO recommends both for PTSD; both need specialist training and supervision. | S34, S2 |
| Diagnosing, or writing a diagnosis in a report | Outside scope for a PSC | Screening yes, diagnosis by a psychiatrist or registered clinical psychologist. | S52, S1 |
| Advising on medicine doses, starting, stopping or switching | Outside scope | Refer to the prescriber; support adherence and report side effects. | S1 |
