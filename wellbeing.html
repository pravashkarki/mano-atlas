Psychosocial wellbeing & intervention

"Psychosocial" is one word on purpose: the psychological (thoughts, emotions, behaviour) and the social (family, community, economy, culture) shape each other constantly. Intervention means acting on both sides at once, and mostly through communities, not clinics.

The pyramid of need and intervention

CTEVT PSC curriculum

CTEVT PSC curriculum: on the official Psychosocial Counselor syllabus, can appear in the exam. Beyond curriculum: added for completeness, not examinable. A chip like 296.2x · F32 is the diagnosis code: the DSM-5's older ICD-9-CM number, then the ICD-10 "F-code" used in hospital records. Non-students can ignore all of these.

Core ideas the curriculum names: psychosocial wellbeing has dimensions (biological, emotional, social, cultural, spiritual, material); intervention can be individual or community-based (CBI): CBI mobilises the community's own resiliency (groups, rituals, leaders) rather than importing outside experts; a rights-based approach treats women's, children's (CRC) and elders' basic rights as the floor of wellbeing; and a multidisciplinary holistic approach means the counselor, health worker, teacher, lawyer, and social worker act as one linked system, which is why referral, coordination and linkage get their own page (25).

The layers around a person, and what wellbeing is made of

Bronfenbrenner's social-ecological model reads a life as rings around the person. A counselor who only works the innermost ring misses where most Nepali distress is made and where much of it can be eased.

RingWhat sits thereA Nepali example
IndividualTemperament, health, coping style, personal historyA quiet girl who somatises worry as stomach pain
MicrosystemSettings the person is directly in: family, school, workplace, peersA joint household; a teacher who calls the child "lato"
MesosystemLinks between those settingsWhether the school ever talks to the parents
ExosystemSettings that shape life without the person being in themThe father's job in Qatar; the ward office; local media
MacrosystemCulture, law, economy, shared beliefsCaste, dowry expectations, karma explanations, the migration economy

What wellbeing is made of (the elements the curriculum lists): psychological: self-acceptance, autonomy, environmental mastery (feeling able to handle daily life), purpose, personal growth, emotional self-regulation · social: warm relationships, belonging, contributing something, trust and safety in one's community, cultural and spiritual connectedness · structural: physical safety, food and housing and livelihood, access to health, education and legal protection. The bottom row is the floor the other two stand on.

Community-based support: principles and a roadmap

CTEVT PSC curriculum

A chip like 296.2x · F32 is the diagnosis code: the DSM-5's older ICD-9-CM number, then the ICD-10 "F-code" used in hospital records. Non-students can ignore all of these.

Community-based MHPSS treats the community as the agent of its own recovery, not the audience for outside experts. Its principles: do no harm (no imported stigma, no disrupted local structures) · meaningful participation of all ages, genders and abilities · build local capacity (teachers, health workers, leaders) · cultural fit with local beliefs and healing traditions · inclusion of the marginalised. Modalities you will use: Psychological First Aid (PFA & emergencies), psychoeducation, structured groups, task-shared protocols such as WHO's Problem Management Plus (PM+), and livelihood support joined to peer groups.

StepWhat you doLooks like
1 · Assess with the communityFocus groups, key informants, a map of assets and existing copingWho do people already go to? Mothers' groups, the health post, the jhankri
2 · Co-designAdapt materials with a local committee: language, metaphors, timing"Man" not "mental health" on the poster; sessions after the harvest
3 · Build capacity, share tasksTrain gatekeepers and volunteers in basics, PFA, groups, safe referralFemale community health volunteers who can spot risk and refer
4 · Run core activitiesPeer groups, child-friendly spaces, cultural and recreational eventsA women's circle; a safe space in the school after the flood
5 · Referral pathwaysLink to the health system for those who need layer 3 or 4 careA named person and number at the nearest hospital
6 · Monitor togetherTrack outcomes with validated tools and community feedbackNepali PHQ-9 or GAD-7 at start and end; a review meeting the group owns
Key points
  1. Psychosocial means mind and social world shaping each other; intervention works on both at once.
  2. The IASC pyramid: most people need only basic services and community support; the counselor works in the third layer; few need specialists.
  3. Community-based support builds on what the community already has: do no harm, involve everyone, train local people.
