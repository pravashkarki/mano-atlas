# HIV & AIDS counselling

HIV is now a manageable chronic condition: a person on treatment lives a near-normal lifespan, and effective treatment with a sustained undetectable viral load makes the virus sexually untransmittable (U=U). The counselor's battle is therefore mostly with stigma, disclosure, and adherence, not the virus.

## Facts, VCT, and the psychosocial load

**Transmission: only four routes:**

- Unprotected sex with an infected person
- Infected blood (transfusion, shared needles)
- Mother-to-child (pregnancy, birth, breastfeeding: largely preventable with treatment)
- Contaminated sharp instruments
**Not transmitted** by touch, sharing food or utensils, mosquitoes, toilets, or living together: the facts that dissolve most household stigma. HIV (the virus) is not AIDS (the late, untreated stage); with antiretroviral therapy most people never reach AIDS.

**VCT: voluntary counselling and testing** is the curriculum's named service.

- *Pre-test counselling:* informed consent, what the test can and cannot say (window period), risk review, preparing for either result
- *Post-test:* a negative result is a prevention conversation; a positive result is delivered simply and humanely, sitting with the shock, assessing suicide risk, planning who to tell and how, and linking to ART the same week
- *The 5 C's:* consent, confidentiality, counselling, correct results, connection to care
Nepal

Concentrated epidemic: key populations include people who inject drugs, sex workers and their clients, men who have sex with men, and returned migrant workers and their wives: the last group is why HIV counselling belongs in every district, not just cities. ART is free at government centres. Stigma ("HIV = immorality") still blocks testing; your confidentiality practice is the counterweight. Ethical note from the ACA code: disclosure to an identifiable at-risk partner is a last resort, only after working with the client toward voluntary disclosure: take such cases to supervision.

The VCT flow. The two highlighted boxes are where the counselor lives: before the test (consent, what a result would mean) and after it (the result, plainly, with the next step in hand). Everything else is the health system's job.

Key points

1. HIV is a manageable chronic condition; treatment gives a near-normal lifespan, and a sustained undetectable viral load means no sexual transmission (U=U).
2. Only four routes transmit it; touch, food, mosquitoes and living together do not. These facts dissolve most stigma.
3. The counselor's work is testing with consent (VCT), disclosure, adherence and stigma, not the virus itself.
