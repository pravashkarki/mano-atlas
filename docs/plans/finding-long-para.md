# Plan: finding-long-para

This work exists because 10+ pages contain paragraphs exceeding the roughly 70-word ceiling from the VOICE-TONE scannability rules, and a reader on a phone viewport cannot scan a wall of text to find the actionable idea.

## Premise check

`docs/VOICE-TONE.md` line 50 states: "No paragraph over roughly 70 words. A wall of text is a writing failure, not a reader failure: split it, or the idea is not understood yet." The audit ledger at `review/learning-ux-audit.md` confirms long-para findings across casemgmt (Documentation ~90 words, Linkage ~110 words), nepal (healing-systems ~120 words), pfa (PFA is not ~80 words), gbv (core distinctions ~90 words, Nepal context ~90 words), hiv (VCT ~90 words, Nepal context ~90 words), ethics (self-care ~100 words, confidentiality ~80 words), skills (SOLER ~100 words), skills-moving (challenge ~85 words), process (card 3 prose ~100 words), techniques (ERP ~80 words), and suicide (assessment ~90 words). The rule is a house standard, not a build gate; nothing currently enforces it at build time. Each affected paragraph is a unit of thought that has grown beyond what a reader can hold on a phone.

## What ships

- Each affected paragraph is split at clause boundaries into shorter paragraphs, each under 70 words.
- The split preserves the paragraph's meaning; no ideas are removed, reworded, or re-ordered. Only sentence boundaries are used as split points.
- Subject and verb stay early in each resulting paragraph, per the VOICE-TONE and lds-writing rules.
- The split does not create one-sentence paragraph runs; a short paragraph lands because a longer one comes before it (lds-writing general.md section 3).
- No paragraph on any affected page exceeds 70 words after the pass.
- The pass is an editorial change to `content/<slug>.html` files; no build.py changes are required unless a content-based enforcement tool is added.

## Cases and decisions

- Split at clause boundaries (periods, semicolons, colons), not mid-sentence. A sentence is a complete thought; splitting inside one breaks the reader's model.
- If a paragraph is 75 words, it may need one split into two. If it is 120 words, it likely needs three or four. The number of resulting paragraphs is whatever the idea requires.
- A paragraph that is exactly 70 words is acceptable; the rule is "over roughly 70 words." Open decision: whether to tighten to a hard 70-word cap or keep the "roughly" qualifier. Recommend keeping "roughly" to avoid false-precision anxiety in the editorial pass.
- A paragraph under 70 words that is a single long sentence with a compound structure is acceptable; the rule targets word count, not sentence count.
- Crisis pages (suicide, pfa, gbv) already use short sentences; the long paragraphs found are in the explanatory prose between the crisis content and the Quick check, not in the crisis cards themselves.
- The `secsub` description paragraphs and the `gentle` reading note are generated by build.py; if they exceed 70 words, the fix goes in the source string in `build.py`, not in `content/`.
- Nepali text length mirrors the English (the build counts English words for reading time); the split must apply to both languages. Since content is bilingual, splitting the English also splits the Nepali because they share the same sentence boundaries.

## Security

- Splitting paragraphs does not change any factual claims; the content is preserved, only re-formed.
- No new strings are introduced, so no new en/ne pair obligations.
- The fix touches only `content/<slug>.html` files; no build logic, CSS, or JS changes.
- Crisis content paragraphs must retain their calm, reassuring tone after splitting; a split that creates a jarring short sentence is a bad split.
- No invented facts, numbers, or quotes are introduced during the rewrite.

## Validation

- A word-count script or manual check confirms every paragraph on every affected page is 70 words or fewer.
- A grep for the dash character across content returns nothing; no em-dashes are introduced by the splits.
- The page reads aloud as a series of short, connected thoughts; no run of one-sentence paragraphs that reads as disconnected.
- Each affected page passes the "one idea per paragraph" test: every paragraph after the split expresses one complete thought.
- `python3 build.py` runs clean; no new gate failures.
- The longest paragraph on any affected page is confirmed under 70 words by measurement, not assumption.

## Rollout

An editorial pass, not a build change. The affected pages are listed in the audit ledger; the pass proceeds page by page in the order casemgmt, nepal, pfa, gbv, hiv, ethics, skills, skills-moving, process, techniques, suicide, plus any others found by a fresh scan. Each page is one commit on `dev`, preview on Vercel, and the word-count check is verified before the commit lands. No build tool change is required for the pass itself; whether to add a build-time enforcement is an open decision recorded below. The pass is a single phase; all pages ship together once verified.
