# Counselling: approaches, forms & what it is

Counselling is a structured, confidential helping relationship in which a trained person helps a client understand their situation and find their own way through it. It is NOT advice-giving, lecturing, interrogation, or ordinary chat, and the client, not the counselor, remains the expert on their own life.

## Six approaches: one table

| Approach | Problem is… | Counselor does… | Best for · watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psychodynamic | Unconscious conflict rooted in early relationships | Listens for patterns, explores childhood, works with the relationship itself | Long-standing relational patterns · slow, needs training |
| Behavioural | Learned behaviour kept alive by reinforcement | Exposure, activity scheduling, reinforcement of new behaviour | Phobia, avoidance, habits · ignores meaning if used alone |
| Humanistic (Rogers) | Growth blocked by lack of acceptance | Empathy, genuineness, unconditional positive regard; client leads | The foundation of ALL counselling · not enough alone in severe illness |
| Cognitive-behavioural (CBT) | Unhelpful thoughts drive feelings and behaviour | Identify → test → reframe distortions; homework; behavioural experiments | Depression, anxiety · needs literacy-adapted tools in Nepal |
| Exposure technique | Avoidance keeps fear alive (the loop of [Fear: the foundation](https://manoatlas.com/foundation.md)) | Graded ladder; stay long enough to learn that the feared outcome is less likely or more bearable than predicted (anxiety may or may not fall); repeat | Phobia, OCD; PTSD only as trauma-focused therapy by a trained provider · never force; consent and pacing are everything |
| Socio-cultural | Distress produced by context: poverty, discrimination, family, culture | Works with family/community, healers, resources, advocacy | Almost every Nepali case · don't let it excuse skipping the individual |

The table is the map a first reader needs. The four sections below open one approach at a time, at the depth the curriculum expects for the exam.

## Seven forms of counselling · three modes

| Form | When | Key precaution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual | The default: one client, private space | Confidentiality; records with codes, not names |
| Family | Problem lives between members (a child's refusal, an elder's care) | Define who the client is; stay neutral between members |
| Group | Shared problems: grief, addiction recovery, GBV survivors | Screen members; group confidentiality agreement |
| Crisis | Immediately after acute events; safety first, feelings second | Short, practical, directive is OK here |
| Supportive | Ongoing emotional support where cure is not the goal (chronic illness, caregivers) | Don't promise change; prevent dependency |
| Couple / marriage | Relationship distress | Balance; screen for violence FIRST: couple work is unsafe amid GBV |
| Task-oriented | One concrete goal (exam panic, a decision) | Keep scope narrow; finish and close |

**Three modes** (know the difference for the exam):

- *Advice-oriented*: telling what to do. Sometimes needed in crisis, but not counselling proper.
- *Informative*: giving accurate information, such as HIV facts or service directions.
- *Psychological or psychosocial counselling*: the real craft, helping the client find their own solution.
Most clients arrive expecting advice. Part of session one is teaching what counselling is.

Key points

1. Counselling is a structured, confidential relationship that helps people find their own way; it is not advice or lecturing.
2. Six approaches (psychodynamic, behavioural, humanistic, cognitive-behavioural, exposure, socio-cultural) each explain suffering differently and offer different tools; the humanistic conditions underlie all of them.
3. Forms of counselling (individual, group, family, crisis, peer...) are chosen by the problem and the setting, not by habit.
